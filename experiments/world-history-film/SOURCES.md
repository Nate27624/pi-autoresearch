## Research rules

1. Tie every nontrivial quantified or historically contested claim to a specific marker. Editorial metaphors and explicitly labeled opinions do not need pseudo-citations.
2. Search for disconfirming evidence; distinguish strong consensus, reasonable scholarly disagreement and unknown origins.
3. No one-region-as-universal-progress narratives. Specifically strengthen Oceania, Africa, Indigenous Americas, South/SE Asia, and gender/class perspectives in the next research rounds.
4. **Never silently change historical numbers or dates**; log the revision, evidence, and reason.
5. No claim that AI is an eyewitness, a neutral oracle, or the authority over human historians.
6. Final film credits should list the sources used for factual claims and separate licensed real footage from AI-generated visualizations.

## Known claim-coverage gaps

- Precise chronology of first farming in different regions, multiple independent writing traditions.
- Reliable primary scholarship for Pacific navigation, Atlantic exchange, Black Death, Islamic and African intellectual histories.
- Dated references for industrial change, abolition movements, World War I, atomic bombings, and COVID-19.
- AI capabilities, impacts and risks evolve rapidly and require explicit source dates.
- Visual licensing, permissions, museum image rights and archival provenance require independent verification.
